# Data Visualisation & Cross-Dataset Analysis - Music & Mental Health Datasets

## Business Problem
This project examines whether age, listening frequency, and music genre preferences are linked to mental health outcomes, helping determine how music streaming habits may support emotional wellbeing.
Our primary question explores: _How does the age, music listening frequency and preferred music genres of people across the globe with specific mental health issues relate to music preferences and mood improvement?_


## Dataset
Analysed 1,000+ user data using combined Kaggle datasets to assess how music listening behaviour impacts mental health outcomes across 20+ variables. Designed relational database architecture (ERD, LDM), integrated multi-source data using SQL, and built Tableau dashboards to uncover behavioural segmentation patterns. Wiht global streaming trends and music & mental health surveys, the analysis connects listening behaviors, genre preferences, and mental health scores to better understand how music influences psychological wellbeing.

## Key Findings
- Age: Teens listened the longest and showed higher mental health concerns, while mental health issues generally declined with age, making younger groups the key focus for analysis.
- Freqeuncy: Longer listening duration was associated with higher mental health issue scores, and those reporting positive music effects had the highest listening time, suggesting immersive listening may improve emotional regulation.
- Music Genres: Gospel and Lofi showed the strongest positive therapeutic effects, Pop and Video game music showed the most negative outcomes, and genre benefits varied by age (e.g., Classical for young adults, Hip-hop/Folk/Jazz for teens).
- Listener and Membership Type: Casual listeners were already close in engagement to super listeners, indicating strong potential for conversion through more targeted music preference strategies.


## Business Recommendations
Age, listening habits, and music genre are key indicators for streaming platforms targeting specific audiences. Music can positively affect mental health, but therapeutic outcomes depend heavily on genre selection. Younger listeners, especially those with mental health concerns, represent a high-value target group, as targeted genre promotion can improve wellbeing while converting casual users into more engaged subscribers.
