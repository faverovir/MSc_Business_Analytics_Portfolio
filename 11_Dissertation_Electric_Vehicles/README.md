# Powering the Transition: Trade Shifts, Critical Minerals & Policy Dynamics in the Global EV Market

## Business Problem
This dissertation analysed how government policy, critical mineral supply chains, charging infrastructure, and international trade dynamics influence the competitiveness of the global electric vehicle (EV) market. Focusing on the European Union, the United States, and China, the study aimed to understand how the transition from crude oil to critical minerals is reshaping global trade patterns and what strategic actions policymakers can take to strengthen long-term EV competitiveness.


## Dataset
The research combined multiple international datasets from publicly available sources, including:
- International Energy Agency (Global EV Outlook)
- Harvard Atlas of Economic Complexity
- Statista
- World Bank and OECD reports
- Government policy publications (EU, US & China)

The analysis covered EV adoption, charging infrastructure, international trade flows for crude oil and critical minerals (lithium, cobalt, nickel, manganese and graphite), production costs, investment activity, and policy measures across the three major EV markets.


## Methods Used
### Data Collection & Preparation
Data from multiple international sources was collected, cleaned, standardised, and integrated into a unified analytical framework. Trade flows, EV prices, infrastructure, and policy variables were aligned across regions to enable cross-country comparisons.

### Statistical Analysis
Spearman correlation analysis and multivariate regression models were used to evaluate relationships between EV prices, critical mineral trade, crude oil exports, and market performance across China, the EU, and the US.

### Machine Learning & Forecasting
Forecasting models were developed in RStudio using:
- ARIMA Time Series
- Random Forest
- Decision Trees
- Boosted Regression Trees
- Support Vector Regression
- K-Nearest Neighbours

Models were evaluated using forecasting accuracy metrics and residual diagnostics to project future trade flows of crude oil and critical minerals.

### Comparative Policy Analysis
Government interventions—including subsidies, tax incentives, infrastructure investment, industrial policy, and foreign direct investment—were analysed across China, the EU, and the United States to assess their impact on EV adoption and international competitiveness. 


## Key Findings
- Government policies were the strongest driver of EV market growth, significantly influencing consumer adoption, production costs, and foreign investment.
- China maintains a substantial competitive advantage through integrated critical mineral supply chains, battery production, industrial policy, and charging infrastructure.
- Well-developed charging infrastructure strongly correlates with higher EV adoption, highlighting infrastructure investment as a critical enabler of market growth.
- The transition from crude oil towards critical minerals is reshaping international trade flows and creating new geopolitical dependencies.
- Regression analysis identified statistically significant relationships between EV prices and critical mineral exports, particularly in China, while forecasting models project continued structural shifts in commodity trade linked to EV adoption. 


## Business Recommendations
Governments should prioritise long-term industrial strategies that combine targeted subsidies, domestic battery manufacturing, and investment in charging infrastructure rather than relying solely on consumer incentives. Diversifying critical mineral supply chains and strengthening international partnerships can reduce geopolitical risk while improving supply security. Policymakers should also support innovation in battery technologies, recycling, and circular economy initiatives to reduce dependence on scarce raw materials and enhance long-term competitiveness. Overall, sustained policy coordination between trade, infrastructure, and industrial development will be essential for accelerating the global transition to electric mobility.
