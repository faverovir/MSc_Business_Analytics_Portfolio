# ESG Ratings vs Societal Impact – Case: BAT 
Led a strategic ESG analysis of British American Tobacco (BAT) to evaluate misalignment between reported ESG ratings and real societal impact, identifying systemic “harm substitution” across geographies and product lines. Developed an ESG Misrepresentation Index (EMI) using AHP weighting, controversy analysis, and sustainability gap modelling to expose reporting blind spots such as Scope 3 health externalities and deliver policy recommendations for investors, regulators, and ESG framework reform.

## Business Problem
This project examined whether British American Tobacco’s (BAT) improving ESG scores genuinely reflect reduced societal harm or instead reveal structural flaws in mainstream ESG frameworks. The core challenge was to evaluate the disconnect between reported ESG performance and real-world impact by identifying whether BAT’s “A Better Tomorrow” strategy represents true harm reduction or “harm substitution” through youth nicotine recruitment, geographic risk transfer, and underreported product-level externalities. The objective was to build a materially grounded ESG Misrepresentation Index (EMI) that exposes where disclosure-based ESG systems may overrate controversial industries while overlooking core societal harm.


## Dataset
The analysis combined multi-source ESG, financial, operational, and public health datasets from 2018–2025, including BAT annual and sustainability reports, MSCI/Sustainalytics/CDP/ISS/S&P ESG ratings, BAT revenue splits (combustibles vs smokeless), Scope 1–3 emissions data, youth vaping prevalence from ASH UK and CDC, global tobacco production from FAO, Ocean Conservancy pollution data, governance disclosures, and regional revenue distribution (AME, APMEA, US). These datasets were normalised and integrated to compare external ESG ratings with internally adjusted, evidence-based societal harm measures.


## Methods Used
### Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP):
AHP was used to construct a sector-specific ESG weighting framework that rebalanced ESG priorities by assigning 50% weight to Social factors, 25% to Environmental, and 25% to Governance. This corrected for traditional ESG underweighting of product-related social harm and prioritised youth vaping, nicotine dependency, and geographic harm transfer.

### ESG Misrepresentation Index (EMI) Gap Analysis:
The EMI quantified the divergence between BAT’s reported composite ESG score and its AHP-adjusted materially weighted ESG score. By comparing disclosure-led ESG ratings against evidence-based harm metrics, the model measured the scale and growth of ESG overstatement over time.

### Forecasting, Predictive Analytics & Optimisation:
The study applied Holt-Winters forecasting, linear emissions trend modelling, exponential revenue crossover modelling, geographic data mining, Lorenz supply concentration analysis, and portfolio optimisation to evaluate BAT’s transition toward smokeless products, long-term revenue resilience, emissions trajectory, and strategic fragility.


## Key Findings
- BAT’s reported ESG score increased from 74.5 (2023) to 81.0 (2025), while its materially adjusted ESG score remained much lower at 39.0 in 2025.
- The ESG Misrepresentation Index (EMI) widened from 39.5 to 42.0, showing that reported ESG improvements outpaced real societal harm reduction.
- Mainstream ESG frameworks appear to overweight disclosure quality and operational metrics while underestimating social harms such as youth vaping, nicotine dependency, and geographic harm transfer.
- BAT’s “harm reduction” strategy more closely reflects harm substitution and market expansion than genuine reductions in societal harm.

## Business Recommendations
This project suggests that ESG ratings alone should not be taken at face value, especially in industries where the product itself creates significant societal harm. Investors, regulators, and ESG providers need to look beyond polished sustainability reporting and focus more on real-world outcomes such as dependency reduction, youth protection, and whether harm is actually being reduced rather than shifted elsewhere. For companies like BAT, building genuine credibility will require proving measurable societal improvement, not just stronger disclosures. More broadly, ESG frameworks should become more practical, transparent, and industry-specific so they better reflect how businesses truly impact people and society.
