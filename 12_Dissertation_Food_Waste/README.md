# Using Data Analytics to Optimise Retail Food Waste: A Cost & Efficiency Perspective in the UK

## Business Problem

This dissertation analysed how business analytics can help UK food retailers reduce food waste, improve operational efficiency, and lower waste-related costs. Using the Co-operative Group (Co-op) as the primary case study, the research investigated whether reductions in food waste were driven by operational improvements, structural business changes, or external economic factors such as food price inflation. The project aimed to provide retailers with data-driven strategies for measuring performance more accurately and supporting more sustainable supply chain decision-making.



## Dataset
The analysis combined multiple publicly available datasets covering the period 2015–2024, including:
- Co-operative Group annual food waste reports
- WRAP UK food waste statistics
- FAO Food Price Index (monthly commodity prices)
- Tesco food waste reporting (benchmark comparison)
- Ocado operational reports
- UK grocery market statistics and market share data

The datasets were integrated to analyse waste volumes, commodity price inflation, operational scale, and retailer performance across multiple food categories.



## Methods Used

### Data Collection & Feature Engineering
Data from multiple public sources was collected, cleaned, standardised, and integrated into a single analytical framework. Derived variables were created, including:
- Economic cost of food waste
- Waste intensity ratios
- Waste per retail store
- Waste relative to estimated food revenue
- Lagged dairy price inflation variables

These measures enabled meaningful comparisons across retailers and over time.

### Statistical Analysis
Multiple statistical techniques were applied to evaluate the drivers of retail food waste, including:
- Correlation analysis
- Multiple linear regression
- Diagnostic testing
- Time-lag analysis
- Comparative retailer benchmarking

The analysis examined relationships between food waste, commodity prices, operational scale, and energy costs.

### Business Analytics Framework
The research developed a practical analytics framework demonstrating how retailers can use integrated operational and pricing data to improve waste forecasting and decision-making. A relational database schema and SQL implementation were designed to illustrate how internal transactional data could support predictive waste management systems.

### Strategic Business Evaluation
Findings were interpreted through an operational efficiency perspective, distinguishing waste reductions caused by genuine process improvements from those resulting from structural changes such as store closures. This produced practical recommendations for performance measurement, sustainability reporting, and retail decision-making.



## Key Findings
- Dairy products consistently generated the highest food waste costs, with commodity price inflation significantly increasing the financial impact during the 2021–2022 price surge.
- Co-op reduced food waste by approximately 45% between 2015 and 2024; however, much of this reduction was explained by a smaller retail store network rather than operational efficiency alone.
- Food price inflation had only a modest influence on waste reduction once structural business changes were considered.
- Measuring waste using normalised metrics (such as waste per store or waste relative to sales) provides a more accurate assessment of operational performance than absolute waste volumes.
- Retailers should distinguish between efficiency gains and structural business changes when setting sustainability targets and reporting environmental performance.

## Business Recommendations
Retailers should move beyond measuring absolute food waste volumes and adopt operational efficiency metrics that account for changes in business size. Integrating pricing, inventory, and waste data into predictive analytics systems would enable earlier intervention and more accurate waste forecasting. Investment in centralised data infrastructure, automated reporting, and SQL-based monitoring systems can improve inventory planning while reducing unnecessary waste. Finally, sustainability reporting should prioritise waste intensity measures rather than total waste tonnage to provide a clearer picture of operational performance and long-term environmental progress.
